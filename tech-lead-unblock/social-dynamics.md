# Social dynamics (conflict and interpersonal)

Companion to [SKILL.md](SKILL.md). Use when recommendations could affect **who trusts whom**, **what gets attributed to whom**, or **how safe people feel** raising issues.

## Pre-action checklist (expanded)

**Attribution**  
Ask: could this action let someone infer *who* raised a concern or complained? If yes, default to approaches that **do not out the source**: private framing, pattern-based language (“I’ve noticed… generally”), or a broader process change announced without tying it to one report.

**Second-order effects**  
Beyond fixing the immediate issue: could this cause **resentment**, **defensiveness**, **retaliation fears**, or **less willingness** to speak up next time? Name at least one second-order risk before endorsing a move.

**Intent vs perception**  
Good intent does not guarantee good reception. For each party, briefly ask: *how might this land for them given history and power?*

**Intervention ladder (low to high visibility)**  
Prefer, in order: private 1:1; **general** team guidance or working agreement; process or tooling change without naming individuals; only then more visible or corrective steps—and escalation paths when policy or safety requires it.

**System vs person framing**  
Where honest, describe issues as **handoffs, ambiguity, capacity, or missing norms**. Naming individuals as “the problem” in public often creates new damage; reserve direct personal accountability for private management paths.

**Reversibility**  
Favor steps that can be **rolled back** or softened (pilot, time-box, trial norm) before irreversible reputational or structural moves.

**Power dynamics**  
Seniority, tenure, informal influence, and prior conflict all change how the same words land. Avoid suggesting that a junior person “just push back” without scaffolding.

**Signal vs noise**  
Distinguish a **pattern** (repeated, after feedback) from a **one-off** stress reaction. Calibrate response to severity and repeatability.

## Reality check

- The model is **not in the room**: it does not hear tone, see body language, or know unspoken history unless you provide it.
- **You** supply missing context and judge what the team can absorb.
- Treat suggested actions as **structured options**, not commands. **You** are the final filter on what is socially safe in your culture.

## Short example (attribution and ladder)

**Weaker approach:** Announce a new team rule the day after Y confides that X’s behavior is a problem. Even without naming Y, X may infer the source, and trust between X, Y, and you can suffer.

**Stronger approach:** Address X in a **private** 1:1 using **pattern-based, non-attributed** language (“I’ve noticed reviews have been blunt in a way that lands badly—let’s align on how we give feedback”). If a team norm is needed, introduce it as a **general improvement** for everyone, not as punishment tied to one incident—unless escalation policy already requires a different path.

When stakes are high or you are unsure, use **`grill-me`** on the plan before you act.
